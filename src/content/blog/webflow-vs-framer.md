[Webflow](https://webflow.com/) and [Framer](https://framer.link/unrootdesign) are often mentioned together, but they solve different problems. The real question is what kind of site you are building and who will maintain it later.

## Ease of use — Framer is easier to start, Webflow is harder but deeper

![](/cms/Framer-And-Webflow-Interface-Сompare.jpg)

### Why [Framer](https://framer.link/unrootdesign) feels easier

- Simpler interface
- No need to think about classes or divs
- Very similar to [Figma](https://www.figma.com/)

> “Framer abstracts away some of the code knowledge that Webflow forces you to use.”

### Why Webflow feels harder at first

- Requires understanding HTML/CSS concepts
- More controls and options
- Developer-style thinking

> “Webflow feels like a faster way to write HTML and CSS visually.”

## Technical flexibility — Webflow allows more complex builds

![](/cms/Framer-And-Webflow-Strengths.jpg)

Webflow is more mature and offers fewer technical limitations.

> “There were simple things I could do in Webflow that I wasn’t able to do in Framer.”

## CMS comparison — Webflow CMS is more robust

![](/cms/Create-New-CMS-Collection.jpg)

- More field types
- Reference fields
- Embedded code inside CMS items

## Animation — Webflow scales better for complex motion

> “Animation-heavy websites are easier to build on Webflow.”

## Pricing and hosting — mostly similar

![](/cms/Pricing-And-Hosting-Сompare.jpg)

- Both charge per site hosting
- Both charge per team seat
- Similar pricing once CMS is used

## Who should use which platform

![](/cms/What-Is-Better-To-Choose-Сompare.jpg)

### Choose Webflow if:

- You need scalability
- You need a strong CMS
- You build complex or animation-heavy sites

### Choose Framer if:

- You are designer-first
- You want speed and simplicity
- You build marketing or portfolio sites

Webflow fits complex, scalable projects. [Framer](https://framer.link/unrootdesign) fits fast, design-led builds. The right choice depends on the project and the person building it.
